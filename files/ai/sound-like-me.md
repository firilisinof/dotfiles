## Instructions

Rework the input text so it sounds like a first-person working journal entry. Keep the meaning intact; change only how it sounds and how paragraphs are built. Do not add content, do not remove substantive information.

### Voice
- First person. Past tense for events, present for reflections.
- Conversational but focused. No rhetorical flourish, no posturing.
- Assume the reader already knows the domain. Leave jargon and terms unexplained, when not explicitly explained.

### Sentences
- Short to medium, 8 to 20 words. Favor simple and compound forms.
- Start sentences directly with the subject most of the time: "He told me...", "I had...", "She said that...".
- Use fragments for emphasis or distillation: "Be more creative." / "Just that."
- Minimal connective tissue. New ideas often start a new sentence with no transition word.

### Paragraphs
- One tight paragraph per topic, two if needed. Use a bulleted list only when the content is genuinely enumerable.
- No topic sentences. Let the paragraph unfold chronologically or associatively, mirroring how the event or conversation actually went.
- Close topics with a short intent or judgment sentence when it fits: "Now, I need to determine the next steps." / "I told him no problem, but I would check with X."

### Reported speech
- Summarize conversations in indirect speech. Almost never quote.
- Extract the takeaway, not the exchange.

### Meta-commentary
- Use small signposts sparingly: "Let me explain.", "Let's take X as an example.", "Late update.", "I had an interesting idea there."

### Diction
- Plain, functional vocabulary.
- Mix in casual phrases naturally: "voilà", "kind of", "just that", "no problem", "totally possible", "out of the box".
- Approximate when precision isn't needed: "around 70%", "less than 1%", "months ago".
- Parenthetical clarifications for names or terms: "(like a private health insurance)".

### Non-native English markers (preserve, do not correct)
- Light seasoning, not every sentence:
  - "suggested me to" instead of "suggested that I".
  - "discussed about" instead of "discussed".
- Always use American English.

### Punctuation
- Standard punctuation. No em-dashes. No semicolons.
- Oxford comma not used. Default to "A, B and C".
- Colons introduce explanations or lists.
- Contractions are fine.

### Avoid
- Em-dashes, semicolons, fancy connectives ("furthermore", "moreover", "consequently").
- Direct quotations of conversations.
- Topic sentences and formal paragraph structure.
- Explaining jargon or translating foreign terms.
- Hedging filler ("it is worth noting that", "in conclusion").
- Over-polished, native-sounding English.

### Self-check before returning
1. Are sentences mostly short and subject-first?
2. Is there at least one fragment used for emphasis?
3. Is reported speech indirect, never quoted?
4. Are there one or two light non-native markers, no more?
5. Did I avoid em-dashes and semicolons?
